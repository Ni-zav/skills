# Lifecycle flows, cadence, and welcome delivery

Read when building a multi-step journey or deciding how often and when to mail.

## Flow design

| Flow | Entry | Exit/stop condition | Design |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Welcome | Confirmed eligible subscription | Welcome delivered, suppression, or expiry | Deliver promised asset promptly; explain cadence; give one early win |
| Newsletter | Active eligible preference | Opt-out, suppression, preference change | Repeatable recognizable format; useful lesson; contextual optional next step |
| Nurture | Declared interest or meaningful event | Converted, inactivity threshold, opt-out | Address one objection or use-case per message; avoid repetitive pitch |
| Promotion | Product/event fit and eligible audience | Deadline, purchase, opt-out | Verified offer, accurate terms, credible urgency, clear CTA |
| Re-engagement | Low recent human engagement after defined period | Reconfirmed interest or sunset | Small eligible cohort, low frequency, clear preferences; respect suppression |
| Transactional | Actual service/user event | Event resolved | Operational information primary; do not use as a workaround to send marketing |

## Practical sequence planning

For each step record: trigger, eligible cohort, time window, problem, promised value, verified proof, CTA, suppression/exclusion conditions, and a stop rule. Prevent simultaneous sequences from over-emailing one recipient.

The source video suggests starting small (even monthly), eventually twice-monthly or more, and testing approximate twice-weekly or three-times-weekly frequency. Treat all of these as **speaker experience**, not a required cadence. Choose based on promise, audience feedback, production quality, sender reputation, and observed incremental value.

Its cited weekday/time-of-day 'winners' are hypotheses. Consider recipient local time and organizational working days; test using randomized cohorts and conversion outcomes. Never promise an algorithmic inbox-placement result by picking a weekday.

## Subscription expectations

Capture the subscriber's intended topic and expected frequency. Do not abruptly move a monthly list to daily. Always deliver the lead magnet requested, even if the recipient does not take an extra reply action. Ask for replies only where the question is meaningful and voluntary; do not manufacture engagement to manipulate spam filters.
