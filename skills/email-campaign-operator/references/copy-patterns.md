# Evidence-led email copy patterns

Read when writing or diagnosing weak copy. Adapt to industry, lifecycle, sender voice, and sophistication instead of imposing a universal template.

## Tactical newsletter (value-first)

- **Subject:** Specific benefit or question the content will answer. A recognizable series label may be useful for a genuinely established series.
- **Preview text:** Add complementary information, not a repetition of the subject.
- **First line:** Give an immediate, independently useful observation; do not withhold the promised insight behind a click.
- **Problem:** A concrete costly/frustrating scenario the recipient recognizes.
- **Method:** Exactly one main technique, short enough to try.
- **Example:** Only observed or explicitly illustrative facts. Clearly label fictional examples.
- **CTA:** The next logical action: try it, read a deeper explanation, reply with a relevant question, or consider the aligned offer.
- **P.S.:** Optional short reminder/bonus; never a second unrelated pitch.

## A critique rubric (0 missing, 1 partial, 2 strong)

| Dimension | Strong looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Relevance | Matches a verifiable recipient need and readiness |
| Opening payoff | Useful within the first few lines |
| Specificity | Concrete problem, method, and application |
| Evidence integrity | Claims, proof, and promises are grounded |
| Human voice | Direct, clear, compatible with sender's brand |
| CTA fit | Explicit action and outcome connected to the content |

Pass target: 10/12, with no unsupported claims, deception, consent, or opt-out blockers. This is an internal quality heuristic, not a validated conversion predictor.

## Anti-pattern → fix

- 'We are excited to announce...' → start with the problem solved and what changed.
- 'Revolutionary and guaranteed...' → say exactly what is known and why it matters.
- Arbitrary percentage lift → remove unless the measurement, context, and attribution can be verified.
- CTA pasted across every message → bridge from this message's teaching point to the next action.
- Five competing links → identify primary intent; keep any secondary path meaningfully different.
- Long generic pleasantries → deliver relevant substance, preserving any culturally appropriate greeting.

Text-only and clean, light HTML can both work. Inspect deliverability, accessibility and rendering using actual messages. Do not assume deleting images, restricting link count, or hosting content on Google properties guarantees Primary inbox placement.

## Subject / preview review

Are both accurate when the email is opened? Does the preheader clarify a curiosity gap rather than hide important terms? Is the crucial text visible in mobile truncation? Are urgency, scarcity and recipient names real?
